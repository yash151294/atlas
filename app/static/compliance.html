What is held, for how long, who looked, and how a data subject's rights are served. Pseudonymisation with a separate vault is the design; these are the controls that make it defensible, and the assumptions it rests on.

How the design meets UK GDPR

Controls, principle by principle
principlecontrol in the product
Lawful basis, purpose limitationLegitimate interests for the network and retained corpus, with a balancing test; re-use across engagements is a compatible-purpose question the contributor agreement and source notice must answer (open for the business). Engagement scope is a platform property (OneLake security), not an app filter.
Data minimisationNames, contact details, family, nationality, financial and ID details are removed from every retained copy. Only professional descriptors (role, organisation, relationship) are kept, and only for people described professionally.
Special category and offence data (Art. 9, 10)The gate's second model reads every retained copy for health, political opinion, religion, ethnicity, sexuality, union, biometric or criminal-allegation content about a person; it is listed as a reason on the document and raises the risk score, so a person sees it before it is retained under an Art. 9/10 condition.
Pseudonymisation (Art. 4(5), 32)Stable tokens per person; the token-to-identity map lives in a separate vault table with its own retention clock, access log and reason-required reveal. In production the vault sits in its own Lakehouse with OneLake security.
Storage limitationRaw file deleted N days after approval; vault rows expire after N days; both enforced by a job, both logged.
AccuracyIdentity links above a confidence band go to a person; people are never merged on fuzzy evidence alone; every link is undoable because every mention keeps its provenance.
Rights: access, erasureSubject lookup and erasure on this page, logged. Tokens remain, so the corpus stays consistent while the person becomes unidentifiable.
AccountabilityEvery model call logged with model, prompt version and cost; every approval, restore, reveal and deletion logged with who and why. Evaluation numbers are measured by the evals in the pipeline layer and frozen by the snapshot script.
Processing location, no trainingAnswers, planning, the residual check and embeddings run inside the tenant's Azure subscription in UK South under Microsoft product terms (no training). Extraction, the one step that reads raw names, runs on Anthropic's API under no-training commercial terms; the in-tenancy model was measured and lost recall, and Claude on Foundry (same model, inside the tenancy) waits on quota. The provider is stamped on every call.
What the design assumes (to confirm with the DPO before real data)
  1. Pseudonymised copies are still personal data: a role plus organisation plus dates can single a person out. The vault lowers risk; it does not make the corpus anonymous. Lawful basis must cover the retained copy, not only the vault.
  2. Re-using an engagement's transcripts for later, different engagements is a compatible purpose, and sources and mentioned professionals are told (or Art. 14(5)(b) is documented for people named in passing).
  3. Legitimate interests covers business-contact records of sources and professionally described third parties; contributor mapping is covered by the contributor agreement.
  4. Client contracts allow deliverables and transcripts to be retained and searched across engagements; where they do not, the engagement is excluded or routed by flag before ingestion.
  5. The transfer of raw transcripts to the first-party API for extraction is covered by processor terms and a UK transfer mechanism until Claude on Foundry has quota (an env change).
  6. A DPIA is completed before the first real document, and the measured residual rate on a labelled real sample is accepted as the operating risk.
  7. A descriptor written by a model may be wrong about a real person; every descriptor keeps its source passage so it can be corrected.
